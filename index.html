Scandals · last checked Sept 30, 2026

Brands in court

Who's being sued, and over what. A lawsuit is an accusation, not a verdict. Being sued doesn't prove a company did anything wrong, so read these as developing stories.

Talc · Johnson & Johnson

J&J's baby powder is back in court, one case at a time

Tens of thousands of people allege that talc in J&J's powders was contaminated with asbestos and caused their ovarian cancer or mesothelioma. J&J denies that its talc caused cancer. Its third try at settling everything through bankruptcy was dismissed on March 31, 2025, so it now has to fight the cases in court individually. More than 67,000 federal cases were pending as of April 2026, and juries have since awarded more than $3 billion in mesothelioma verdicts, including a $1.5 billion verdict in Baltimore in December 2025. J&J usually appeals, and some awards have already been cut.

Plot twist: Congress's 2022 cosmetics law (MoCRA) told the FDA to create an asbestos-testing rule for talc cosmetics. The FDA proposed one in December 2024, then withdrew it on November 28, 2025, saying it plans to reissue it. For now there's still no standard federal testing requirement.

Ongoing · trials continuing
Sources: JD SupraInsurance JournalPerkins Coie
Hair relaxers · L'Oréal and other makers

The hair relaxer lawsuits

More than 11,000 federal lawsuits, consolidated in Illinois, allege that chemical hair straighteners caused uterine and endometrial cancer. The suits point to formaldehyde, phthalates and other hormone-disrupting chemicals. The companies deny the claims. Discovery is still going, and jury trials aren't expected until 2027 at the earliest. Separately, the FDA proposed banning formaldehyde in hair relaxers in October 2023.

Ongoing · pre-trial
Sources: Van Law Firm (MDL tracker)NPR
Benzene · Neutrogena, Aveeno, Banana Boat

The benzene in the spray can

In 2021, the independent lab Valisure reported finding benzene, a known carcinogen, in dozens of sunscreen and after-sun products. J&J recalled five Neutrogena and Aveeno aerosol sunscreens in July 2021, and Banana Boat recalled a Hair & Scalp spray in July 2022. Class actions followed. In June 2024, an appeals court sent the Neutrogena settlement back for another look at its payouts and attorney fees.

Lab note: benzene was a contaminant, most likely from the aerosol propellant. It was never on the ingredient list, which is why no label checker (including this one) could have caught it.

Recalled · settlement under review
Sources: AWKO LawCellino Law
PFAS "forever chemicals" · CoverGirl, Almay, Burt's Bees

Forever chemicals in waterproof mascara?

A 2021 University of Notre Dame study found markers of PFAS in many waterproof and long-wear products. In 2022, shoppers sued CoverGirl, Almay and Burt's Bees for not disclosing PFAS. The CoverGirl case was dismissed in March 2024 because the plaintiffs couldn't show that the mascaras they personally bought contained PFAS. This is the hard part of these cases: proving what was in your own tube.

CoverGirl case dismissed · others vary

Feature · last checked Sept 30, 2026

To wear or not to wear: the great sunscreen debate

Your feed says sunscreen is poison. Your dermatologist says wear it every single day. Here's what the research actually shows.

What the feed says

“Sunscreen causes cancer”

Anti-sunscreen influencers say chemical filters are toxic, that anything you can't eat shouldn't go on your skin, and that sunshine is all upside.

What the research says

UV causes cancer. Sunscreen lowers the risk.

There's no evidence that sunscreen causes skin cancer. UV rays are a proven carcinogen. Consistent sunscreen use has been linked to about 50% lower melanoma risk and about 40% lower squamous cell carcinoma risk.

The real debate: which one, not whether

Mineral vs. chemical

Mineral filters (zinc oxide, titanium dioxide) sit on the skin. They're the only two filters the FDA proposed in 2019 as "generally recognized as safe and effective."

Chemical filters (like oxybenzone, avobenzone and homosalate) absorb into the skin. FDA studies published in JAMA in 2019 and 2020 found they reach the bloodstream at levels above the FDA's cutoff for requiring more safety testing. That cutoff triggers more research. It doesn't mean the filters are harmful. The FDA has asked manufacturers for more data on 12 chemical filters and hasn't called any of them dangerous.

Lab note: UVA = aging, UVB = burning. "Broad spectrum" means it blocks both.

Sources: Covington (FDA 2019 rule summary)WTOP / AP on the FDA study
Scandal · Australia, 2025

When SPF 50 tested as SPF 4

In June 2025, the Australian consumer group CHOICE tested 20 sunscreens labeled SPF 50 or 50+, and 16 of them came in below their label. Ultra Violette's Lean Screen tested at SPF 4, and it was recalled in August 2025. Several other brands pulled products after that. In the US, the FDA sent warning letters in July 2025 to several companies, including Supergoop, over whipped and mousse-style sunscreens.

Sources: CHOICECosmeticsDesign AsiaManaged Healthcare Executive
New arrival · June 2026

The first new sunscreen filter in over 20 years

In June 2026, the FDA approved bemotrizinol (BEMT). It's a broad-spectrum filter that has been used in Europe and Asia for years. It's very photostable, meaning it doesn't break down fast in sunlight. It's also a large molecule, so less of it is absorbed through the skin.

Sources: HealthDayDermatology Times

The editor's final word

Sunscreen is IN.
  • Broad spectrum, SPF 30 or higher, every day
  • Reapply every 2 hours, and after swimming or sweating
  • Nervous about chemical filters? Go mineral. Just don't go without.
